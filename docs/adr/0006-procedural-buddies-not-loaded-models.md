# Buddies are procedural three.js code, not loaded models

Every body at the table (a seated person, a bot, a standing person, a ghost) is a buddy built entirely in code: a lathe-swept bean with capsule limbs, a face drawn on the body, and an outfit assembled from small geometry builders (hats, hair, facial hair, eyewear, neckwear, tops, footwear, hand props, a shoulder pet). There is no GLB, no texture, no skeleton, no animation clip. Expressions are seven numbers lerped per frame; the mouth is a shape rebuilt when the face changes; walking, jumping, sitting and bouncing are hand-written curves over a handful of joint angles; looking around is eyes first, then the torso. The outfit of a person is derived from the nickname by a seeded draw, so the same nickname looks the same on every screen without anything travelling through the room.

The alternative was modelled characters: a GLB per body with a rig and imported clips, loaded through an asset pipeline. That gives richer silhouettes and free animation, at the cost of an art toolchain, asset hosting and caching, a loader with a loading state, and animation blending code, in a project with no artist and a stated preference for keeping everything as code. The buddies came from a prototype written for exactly this game (`HoldCards`, hand sockets), they fit the existing procedural scene (cards and nametags are already canvas-drawn), and they cost zero dependencies: the model file takes `three` as a plain import and the Threlte wrapper is the same `<T is={group}>` pattern the scene already uses.

## Consequences

Anything that reacts to the game (a truco call, a lost hand, a win) is written against the buddy's face and arm parameters, so switching to modelled characters later means re-authoring every reaction, not just swapping a file. New parts are added by writing a geometry builder, which is cheap for simple shapes and the wrong tool for detailed ones; the look stays deliberately cartoon. Ghosts share one translucent material for every part, so a ghost is recognised by silhouette, not by colour. Because the whole thing is code, it runs headless in tests: the sitting pose, the hand-on-table height and the outfit draw are asserted with `bun test`, which is how this project verifies without a browser.
