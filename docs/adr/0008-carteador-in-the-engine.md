# The dealer, the cut and the deal order are engine state, not scene choreography

Every hand has a carteador: the seat that shuffles and deals. The engine (`packages/rules`) owns it as `GameState.dealer`, draws it from the rng on the first hand of a game (and of every rematch), and passes it one seat to the right on every following hand, so the mão of one hand is the carteador of the next. The mão is derived from it (the seat to the dealer's right), the cutter is the seat to the dealer's left (`cutterOf`), and the cards leave the deck one at a time starting at the mão and going right until every seat holds three (`dealOrder`). The `newHand` event carries `mao`, `dealer` and `cutter`, and the `GameView` that reaches every client carries `dealer`, so the scene animates the shuffle, the cut and the deal from data, and the stock is placed in front of the seat that will deal next. The whole choreography has one fixed length, `DEAL_MS` in the protocol, and a bot's first action of a hand is scheduled after it (on the server and on the local table), so no screen ever sees a bot play before the cards have reached its hand. Nobody presses anything to shuffle, cut or deal.

The alternative was to keep the engine as it was, rotating only the mão, and let the scene derive a dealer from it and play the choreography on its own. That would have left the deal order to each client's guess (the engine dealt seat 0 first regardless of who opened, so a fixed deck landed in different hands than a real deal would), and it would have left the server with no notion of when the deal is over, so bots would keep acting the instant a hand starts while every screen was still dealing. The engine already owns the rotation of the table; the dealer is one more field in that rotation, and the deal wait is one more number the room's pace already knew how to hold.

## Consequences

The first hand of every game opens at a random seat, on the server and offline, so tests that script a whole game fix the dealer (`startHand({ dealer })`, `StepOptions.dealer`) and build their decks with `deckFor(hands, mao)`, which knows the deal order. A rematch draws a new dealer. Bots on the server think a random time (`thinkTime`) instead of a fixed delay, so the room's `pace` became a multiplier on that time plus the deal wait, and tests that assert timer times compute both from the same fixed rng. The scene is now expected to read `dealer` and `cutter` from the event rather than inferring them; a scenery that wants a different deal animation changes how it plays the choreography, never its length, because the server's bots are gated on `DEAL_MS` and would otherwise act while the cards are still in the air.
